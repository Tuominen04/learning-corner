# The library graph

> **Audience:** People using Learning Corner
> **Status:** Implemented
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-14

Once you have more than a handful of pages, a list in a sidebar stops telling you anything.
The **graph** shows the whole library as a picture: every page, how the pages link to each
other, and how the tree branches.

## A picture of what you already wrote

Choose **Graph**, beside **Documents**, **Sources** and **Works**.

Nothing on the graph is something you made for it. Every dot is a page; every line is
something already in your library. So there is nothing to keep up to date: write a page, come
back to the graph, and it is there. Looking at the graph never changes your library.

## What is on it

- **Dots** are your pages. A bigger dot has more lines to it. A colour is one subject: a page
  at the top of the tree and every page under it share one, so a subject reads as one colour
  and the pages that join two subjects stand out.
- **Bright lines** are links: write a link to another page and the line is there.
- **Faint lines** are the tree: each page joined to the page it is under. Turn them off with
  **Show the tree** to see only what you linked yourself.
- **Squares** are sources, with a dashed line from each page that cites one. They are off to
  begin with; turn them on with **Show sources**.

A page with no lines at all sits on its own. That is worth knowing: it is a page nothing
points to yet.

## Looking around

| To do this | Do that |
| ---------- | ------- |
| Move the picture | Drag it |
| Zoom | Scroll, or **+** and **−**. **Fit** shows everything again |
| See what a page is joined to | Point at it: it and its neighbours stand out, and everything else dims |
| Choose a page | Click it, or type part of its title in **Find on the graph** |
| Open a page | Double-click it, or choose it and click **Open this page** |

Choosing a page lists, beside the picture, what it links to, what links to it, the page it is
under, the pages under it, and the sources it cites. Each of those can be clicked to go there,
so you can walk the library from page to page.

From the keyboard: find a page by its title, then the arrow keys move along its lines to the
page that lies that way, and **Enter** opens it.

## When the whole library is too much

Choose a page, then one of **Show only this and**:

- **1 step**, **2 steps** or **3 steps** — the page and everything within that many lines of it.
- **Pages under it** — the page and every page under it, however deep.

**Show everything** brings the rest back. Nothing moves when you narrow the picture or widen
it again, so a page is where you last saw it.

The app remembers how you left the graph — what was switched on, and what it was narrowed to —
for each library on this computer.

## Saving it as a picture

**Save as a picture** saves what is on screen, as it is on screen. **SVG** stays sharp at any
size and its titles can be searched; **PNG** opens anywhere.

## What it is not

The graph cannot be arranged by hand, and it does not hold anything of its own. For a picture
you build on purpose — a family tree, a timeline, a map of a network — see
[Diagrams](diagrams.md).
