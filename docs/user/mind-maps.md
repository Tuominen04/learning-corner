# Mind maps

> **Audience:** People using Learning Corner
> **Status:** Planned
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-06

Once you have more than a handful of documents, a list in a sidebar stops telling you
anything. A **map** shows the same work as a picture: what you have written, and how it
connects.

## A map is a view, not a copy

This is the important part. A map is drawn **from the documents you already have**. A node
on the map *is* a document — open it from the map and you are editing the real thing.

Nothing on a map is a second copy of your writing, so a map cannot drift out of date, and
you can never fix something on the map and find the document unchanged. Delete a map and you
lose only the arrangement; your work is untouched.

## Opening one

Choose **Open map** and it opens in **its own window.** Put it on a second screen, or beside
the document you are writing, and keep it there while you work. It updates as you write.

## What is on it

- **Nodes** — your documents and topics.
- **Lines** — the links between them, drawn from the links already in your documents. Write
  `[[Protein sources]]` in a document and the line appears.
- **Your own lines** — drag from one node to another to connect two documents that ought to
  be related but are not yet, and label the line: *contradicts*, *depends on*, *the same
  thing seen differently*.

You can also add a node that is not a document — a question you have not answered, a thing
you want to read. It sits on the map as a reminder, and can become a real document when you
get to it.

## Getting it arranged

**Arrange automatically** gives you a starting layout. Then move things by hand: your own
arrangement carries meaning that no automatic layout can guess, and the app keeps it.

Filter the map to one topic, or to a distance around one node — *show me this document and
everything within two steps of it* — when the whole library is too much at once.

## More than one map

Keep as many as you like. A map is a point of view, and different questions want different
pictures: one for how a subject fits together, one for what you still need to read, one for
an argument you are trying to construct.

## Sharing it

Export a map as a picture, for a document you are writing or for a wall.
